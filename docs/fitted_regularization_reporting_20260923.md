## Reporting target

There are eight point estimators: compressed or full history, penalty zero or five, and plug-in or DR. Their twelve prespecified paired comparisons are four full-history minus compressed, four shrunk minus raw, and four DR minus plug-in squared-error differences. Every comparison uses the same declared dataset and fold binding within a replicate. A matching identifier checks the supplied record contract; it cannot prove that the underlying fits actually shared their data/folds.

The planned job list defines the denominator before any results are read. Missing records remain unattempted, and attempted, completed and failed states remain explicit. A point estimate can succeed even when its interval calculation fails. Point summaries use valid completed estimates and report their denominator; they are not unconditional performance claims for all planned or attempted fits. Paired differences use only jointly completed point estimates and count every excluded pair. Failed or unattempted slots are never silently removed from the planned ledger. The future runner must durably write an attempted receipt before beginning each job and retain failed/interrupted states: an absent record alone cannot distinguish a never-started slot from lost crash output. The reporter consumes one reconciled final state per slot, not the raw event journal. The future runner must retain that immutable attempt journal and deterministically reconcile state transitions, rather than deleting receipts or passing duplicate states. The omitted-record convention is valid only with that connected logging contract.

Returned-interval coverage divides covering intervals by valid returned intervals. Covering intervals divided by attempted replicates is a separate operational success measure. For example, if ten jobs were planned, eight attempted and six returned intervals, of which five cover, these quantities are5/6 and5/8, with two unattempted jobs retained. Neither ratio is a substitute for the other. Interval failures do not erase valid point estimates, and the reporting component does not manufacture plug-in intervals from prediction dispersion.

Coverage uncertainty uses an explicitly named two-sided95% Clopper–Pearson binomial interval; it is conservative exact inversion under iid replicate-level coverage indicators. The MCSE arithmetic likewise assumes independent replicates from the relevant common law. Neither calculation makes an observed incomplete or selected subset representative. If a cap interrupts the planned study, all summary arithmetic is descriptive of the returned subset and the planned unconditional precision remains unestablished. The plug-in coverage MCSE is zero when observed coverage is zero or one, but the exact binomial interval remains nondegenerate; the zero MCSE is not a certificate of zero uncertainty.
