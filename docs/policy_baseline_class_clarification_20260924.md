# LEAD-POLICY-04: distinguish a fixed recipe from a frozen status rule

Coordinating scientific lead, 24 September 2026. **Prospective comparator-class clarification, not an empirical result or collection release.** The original question is whether a public-history selector improves the next prompt at the *same checkpoint* under the fixed receiver. The unfavorable E11/E12/E13a outcomes and E14 HOLD remain in force.

Our [landmark theory](landmark_prompt_theory.md) defines the best fixed comparator in (L4) as a fixed **candidate index or generation recipe**, not necessarily identical rendered bytes for every task. A frozen recipe may fill task-specific slots under predeclared rules while keeping the same recipe identity across histories. By contrast, a program that selects among different recipes using the public diagnostic is a **fixed-before-evaluation decision rule**, but not a fixed recipe index. These meanings of “fixed” should not be conflated.

The [E12 source audit](policy_fixed_comparator_support_audit_20260924.md) therefore remains correct in its narrower claim: N1 is the only diagnostic-enabled **status-invariant instruction** assigned across its observed statuses; S1 is a predeclared status-dependent rule, not another constant-index candidate with cross-status support. But excluding S1 solely because its output varies would make a claim of superiority over *simple public-history practice* too easy. S1 is a legitimate, fully specified **heuristic rule benchmark** if its public-status function, exact branch texts, checkpoint, receiver, endpoint and costs are locked before untouched evaluation. Its observed E12 −1/28 versus N1 is development evidence for that rule, not a validated policy comparison or proof that it is competent on a target population.

Keep the planned original contrasts distinct: `d` versus a development-selected, supported fixed recipe `b1` tests whether choosing a next-prompt recipe from history adds value over one frozen recipe; `d` versus the independently sampled controller `b2` tests a separate practical comparison under its complete information, stopping and cost contract. A frozen S1-like rule can be a **prespecified secondary benchmark** for whether a learned selector adds value beyond a simple public-status heuristic. It does not silently replace `b1` or `b2`. If superiority over that rule is to be inferential, predeclare its contrast, simultaneous error allocation, precision/futility interpretation and extra branch/resource cap **before** any evaluation outcome. Otherwise report it descriptively, without a practical-superiority conclusion. An `S1` recipe chosen by `d` for a specific history is also not independent evidence of learning.

**Decision: clarify the comparator classes; HOLD new E14/source/model and independent-policy collection.** First freeze a supported recipe set, a development-only `b1` selection rule and the public-feature/training law for `d`; decide prospectively whether the status-rule benchmark is worth its resource cost. The untouched family frame, endpoint, assignment/seed laws, two primary contrasts' inference/useful-gain rules, exact `b2`, cost starting line and numerical local cap remain unresolved. This is a read-only reconciliation of existing theory, renderer and E12 report: zero new receiver calls/tokens, benchmark executions, fits, simulated draws or paid spend. **58%, change 0 percentage points**; prompt efficacy unestablished and the full project not submission-ready.
