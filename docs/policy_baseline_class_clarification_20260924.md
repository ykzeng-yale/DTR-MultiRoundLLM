# LEAD-POLICY-04: distinguish a fixed recipe from a frozen status rule

Coordinating scientific lead, 24 September 2026. **Prospective comparator-class clarification, not an empirical result or collection release.** The original question is whether a public-history selector improves the next prompt at the *same checkpoint* under the fixed receiver. **Correction:** [LEAD-POLICY-05](policy_action_unit_correction_20260924.md) clarifies that S1 itself can be a fixed *recipe index* under the theory's action definition, even though its rendered instruction varies by public status. Its literal strings are not separately cross-status assigned. The unfavorable E11/E12/E13a outcomes and E14 HOLD remain in force.

Our [landmark theory](landmark_prompt_theory.md) defines the best fixed comparator in (L4) as a fixed **candidate index or generation recipe**, not necessarily identical rendered bytes for every task. A frozen recipe may fill task-specific slots or choose among predeclared final strings under a public rule while keeping the same *whole-recipe* identity across histories. A literal constant string, a fixed composite recipe and a selector choosing between recipe indices are distinct action definitions. These meanings of “fixed” should not be conflated.

The [E12 source audit](policy_fixed_comparator_support_audit_20260924.md) now states the precise boundary: N1 and S1 are both same-prefix, diagnostic-enabled **fixed whole-recipe indices** assigned across observed statuses. N1 renders one neutral string; S1 renders a predeclared public-status-dependent string. E12 does not compare each S1 string as a separate fixed literal action across statuses. R1 is constant and diagnostic-enabled but removes the prior answer. S1 can serve as a fixed **composite-recipe** comparator for the narrow N1/S1 slate or as a separately reported simple heuristic when a richer selector is evaluated. Its observed E12 −1/28 versus N1 is development evidence for that whole rule, not validated policy value or proof of competence on a target population.

Keep the planned original contrasts distinct: `d` versus a development-selected, supported fixed recipe `b1` tests whether choosing a next-prompt recipe *index* from history adds value over one frozen whole recipe; `d` versus the independently sampled controller `b2` tests a separate practical comparison under its complete information, stopping and cost contract. If S1 itself is selected as `b1` from a declared development slate, do not count it again as an independent third baseline. If a distinct S1-like heuristic is added beside a different `b1`, declare it as a **secondary benchmark**. Inferential superiority over that extra rule requires its contrast, simultaneous error allocation, precision/futility interpretation and extra branch/resource cap **before** any evaluation outcome. Otherwise report it descriptively. An S1 arm chosen by `d` at one history is not independent evidence of learning.

**Decision: clarify the comparator classes; HOLD new E14/source/model and independent-policy collection.** First freeze a supported recipe set, a development-only `b1` selection rule and the public-feature/training law for `d`; decide prospectively whether the status-rule benchmark is worth its resource cost. The untouched family frame, endpoint, assignment/seed laws, two primary contrasts' inference/useful-gain rules, exact `b2`, cost starting line and numerical local cap remain unresolved. This is a read-only reconciliation of existing theory, renderer and E12 report: zero new receiver calls/tokens, benchmark executions, fits, simulated draws or paid spend. **58%, change 0 percentage points**; prompt efficacy unestablished and the full project not submission-ready.
