# Policy-validation target decision before further E14 spend

Coordinating scientific lead, 23 September 2026. **Prospective design clarification, not a trial freeze, source review or collection release.** The governing question is whether a next-prompt rule based only on permitted public history improves the frozen receiver's expected final private score. This note applies the [existing independent-policy design](independent_prompt_policy_validation_design_20260922.md) and [frame audit](policy_frame_feasibility_20260923.md); it asserts no new estimator, theorem or observed effect. E11–E13 negative development results remain unchanged.

| Choice | Exact claim a completed study could support | Current binding dependency |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Original independent-family policy target** | Family-weighted quality of one frozen public-history selector versus a development-selected competent fixed continuation, plus a separately declared independent-sampling controller, on families drawn under a stated evaluation law. Cost superiority needs an additional total-deployment-cost contrast. | No certified sampling frame/family law, untouched evaluation set, validated endpoint, frozen selector or complete cost/precision/cap contract. The current 198-root convenience frame cannot supply the existing conservative .05-radius target under the optimistic one-family-per-root calculation. A large observed effect might still be detectable; the planning failure is not an impossibility theorem. |
| **B. Finite, source-defined evaluation set** | The realized mean policy contrast over a newly frozen, eligible set of specific roots/families, conditional on the selected source and its validated scoring law. It cannot be reported as the original family-population claim without a sampling/transport argument. | The 198-list includes development-reviewed sources and only mechanical screening for ranks 41–198. It needs a prospectively fixed untouched subset, semantic-family/exposure adjudication, endpoint validation, policy/comparator freeze and an analysis that distinguishes model stochasticity from sampling uncertainty. Changing to this target requires a new labeled hypothesis and rubric judgment, not a quiet relabeling of E14. |
| **C. Fixed instrument feasibility check** | Whether a specified frozen real adapter and scorer preserve assignments, receiver state, private isolation, failure accounting and cost within a small finite run. | Real adapter, grader, token/context, launch/cleanup, immutable trust and host lease remain unvalidated. This check would not estimate prompt efficacy or establish either policy target. It needs its own exact execution cap and cannot inherit the proposed 130-call/228-start E14 allowance. |

**Decision now: HOLD model collection for all three choices.** The E14 ten-history neutral-versus-public-diagnostic-directed arm contrast is a descriptive development comparison on fresh prefixes. A positive result could motivate a selector candidate; a negative result would narrow those instructions at those histories; an inconclusive result would leave their relative performance unresolved. None chooses A versus B, validates a policy, or supplies the missing cost and family law. Spending 130 calls and 228 isolated starts before resolving that decision has no justified advancement criterion. The shortest useful lead-owned next step is to propose one frozen selector and competent fixed/independent-sampling comparators with their decision-time data and full costs, then test whether choice A has a defensible evaluation frame and precision/resource plan. If A is infeasible, explicitly decide whether B is worth a separate finite-target paper claim; C is only justified by a named downstream study it enables.

No new model calls, tokens, benchmark executions, synthetic draws, fitted models, paid spend or worker assignment. **58%, change 0 percentage points** on the fixed completion rubric. Prompt efficacy remains unestablished and the full project is not submission-ready.
