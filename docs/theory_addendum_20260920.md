# Stopping, prefix replay, and candidate selection: a scoped theory addendum

**Date:** 2026-09-20. **Status:** proved under the conditions stated below; exact finite-state checks supplied separately. This addendum supplements [the original theory](theory.md) without replacing its general adaptive-prompt estimand. It reconciles the narrower full-rollout stopping proposal in the experiments draft. Classical change-of-measure and optimal-stopping arguments are not claimed as new foundational results.

## 1. The decision that an unweighted full rollout actually evaluates

Fix a receiver, generator, evaluator, task population, and maximum of `D` continuation decisions. Index artifacts by `k=0,...,D`: artifact zero is the initial receiver answer, and action `A_k` produces artifact `k+1`. This avoids mixing artifact numbers with feedback decisions.

A full rollout always executes the declared continuation kernel `e_k(da|H_k)` at every decision, including histories at which a candidate stopping policy would have stopped. Let `H_k` contain the full recorded prefix, `Y_k` the analyst's score of the current artifact, and `C_k` the cost that accepting that prefix would have incurred. Define the prefix utility

$$
U_k=Y_k-\lambda C_k,\qquad 0\le k\le D.
$$

`C_k` includes the initial call and all calls, checks, and generation already performed before that stop decision, using one declared convention. It excludes calls made later solely for full-rollout data collection. Those later calls are real research expenditure and must still be recorded separately. Prefix replay cannot make an already incurred experimental bill disappear.

Let `\mathcal F_k^O` be the sigma-field generated by **all public observations through decision k**, including the information actually available when deciding to stop. Let `\mathcal F_k^A` additionally contain analyst-only labels recorded by that time. The public fields exclude hidden tests and their derivatives. `\mathcal F_k^O\subseteq\mathcal F_k^A`, and both are increasing filtrations. Writing `\sigma(\varphi_k(H_k))` alone does not ensure a filtration when the feature map forgets previous information; use `\sigma(\varphi_0(H_0),...,\varphi_k(H_k))`, or explicitly restrict a policy to a memoryless feature class.

A frozen stopping rule `\tau\in\{0,...,D\}` is a stopping time for the public filtration: `\{\tau=k\}\in\mathcal F_k^O`. Independent policy randomness can be sampled before the rollout and added to the public filtration. “Frozen” means the rule, feature map, thresholds, and hyperparameters are fixed independently of the evaluation roots. Training on other roots is allowed; conditioning on that training data treats the rule as fixed.

### Assumptions for prefix replay

**S1 — Matching continuation law.** Before stopping, the target policy uses exactly the full-rollout continuation kernel `e`, including generator/class probabilities, within-class text generation, filters, rendering, receiver and tool kernels. Matching a class name while changing the prompt generator is not matching the law. The target continuation itself must be implementable from its allowed information.

**S2 — Prefix consistency and no anticipation.** Executing additional calls after a prefix does not change the artifact, measured outcome, or charged prefix cost already associated with that prefix. Before stopping, the target and collection protocol induce the same conditional distribution of prefixes. A disclosed future horizon, shared mutable files, different truncation, batch contention affecting the target metric, or a receiver instructed that it must complete all rounds can invalidate this condition.

**S3 — Stop outcome is the recorded prefix functional.** Stopping means accepting the current artifact, with utility exactly `U_k` when stopping at `k`. It does not trigger a final rewrite, a cleanup tool, a new judge-dependent selection, deterioration before a fixed later endpoint, or another unrecorded event. Any such effect must be added to the target and measured. A repeatable scorer alone does not establish S2 or S3.

**S4 — Complete observation for the target horizon.** Every needed prefix's public state, grade, and cost is recorded, and all failures have a prespecified measured meaning. The administrative cap is shared by target and collection. Missing continuations or unavailable scores are handled under Section 4 rather than discarded.

**S5 — Honest rule and root sampling.** Rules use only decision-time public information and are frozen outside the evaluation sample. Independent roots, or a declared independent clustering unit, are sampled from the target distribution. A task-weighted target and a family-weighted target are different if family sizes differ.

These are assumptions about a particular data design. They are not consequences of calling STOP an action or of a deterministic verifier.

### Theorem S1: unweighted full-rollout evaluation

Under S1–S4, for any frozen public stopping time `\tau`, the distribution of utility from actually running `e` until `\tau` and accepting that artifact equals the distribution of `U_\tau` read from a full rollout under `e`. Therefore

$$
V(e,\tau)=E_e[U_\tau]
          =E_e\left[\sum_{k=0}^D1\{\tau=k\}U_k\right].
\tag{S1}
$$

Under S5, the root-average estimator is unbiased conditional on training and requires no treatment or stopping likelihood ratios: `W=1`.

**Proof.** Couple target and full-rollout executions using the same initial state and the same exogenous random draws for each continuation, receiver, and tool call. S1 and S2 imply by induction that the two histories are identical through any time before the target stops. Since `\tau` depends only on the public prefix and its presampled randomization, both executions first satisfy the stopping condition at the same prefix. S3 makes the target utility equal to the full rollout's recorded `U_\tau` pathwise. S4 makes that quantity observed. Integrating proves the equality in distribution and (S1). Independent root sampling and a rule fixed outside evaluation yield unbiased averaging. The additional trajectory suffix is unused. ∎

**What “STOP needs no randomization” means.** Under S3, its outcome is an observed prefix functional. No action-assignment probability for STOP is needed to identify that functional. If the logger never selects STOP, its STOP probability is zero: ordinary treatment positivity for STOP does **not** “hold by degeneracy.” Instead a structural observation assumption bypasses the missing STOP action outcome. This is compatible with the original theory's action-based positivity theorem: that theorem did not assume every stop outcome was independently observed along a full continuation.

**What is not necessary.** No independence between prefix grades is required; they may be nonstationary and strongly dependent. Optional stopping does not bias an ordinary sample mean of a bounded, fixed, fully observed path functional. No particular critic architecture is needed. A fixed deterministic grader is convenient but not logically necessary if the target is the same recorded stochastic measurement and measurement randomness is included in the declared data law. It is insufficient to infer a different latent quality or a fresh post-stop noisy measurement without an additional calibration/measurement assumption.

**What is not implied.** The empirical best rule chosen on these same roots has optimistic in-sample value. Complete observation eliminates missing action outcomes for this restricted stopping problem; it does not eliminate overfitting, make hidden scores deployable, establish precision, or prove that a learned policy beats a strong comparator.

## 2. When weights return: a changed continuation policy

Let `\pi_k(da|H_k)` be a different, supported continuation law, and let `\tau` remain a public stopping time. Keep S2–S4 and a common baseline distribution. Define

$$
W_0=1,\qquad
W_k=\prod_{s=0}^{k-1}\frac{\pi_s(A_s\mid H_s)}{e_s(A_s\mid H_s)}.
$$

Only actions executed **before** accepting artifact `k` enter `W_k`; there is no STOP factor.

### Theorem S2: stopped-prefix change of measure

Under sequential support and invariant transition kernels,

$$
V(\pi,\tau)
=\sum_{k=0}^D E_e[1\{\tau=k\}W_kU_k]
=E_e[W_\tau U_\tau].
\tag{S2}
$$

**Proof.** The event `\{\tau=k\}` and `U_k` are measurable in the prefix through `k` (including measurement variables). The likelihood ratio for that prefix under `\pi` versus `e` is `W_k`, since receiver and measurement kernels cancel. Change of measure separately for each event yields `E_\pi[1\{\tau=k\}U_k]=E_e[1\{\tau=k\}W_kU_k]`. Sum the finitely many events. This also proves `E_eW_\tau=1` by setting every `U_k=1`. ∎

If `\pi=e` on all reachable pre-stop prefixes, `W_\tau=1`. Conversely a generic claim of unweighted validity for every bounded prefix outcome requires equality of the corresponding stopped-prefix laws: otherwise the indicator of a measurable set where the laws differ provides a counterexample. For one specific utility, different laws can accidentally have the same mean; thus “only matching continuation” is a sufficient design guarantee and, up to distributional equivalence, necessary for universal replay validity, not an assertion that coincident means are impossible.

**Elementary counterexample.** One continuation draws `A\sim Bernoulli(1/2)`, with final grade `Y_1=A`, and `\tau=1`. Replay gives one half. A target that always chooses `A=1` has value one. Its correct ratio is `2\,1\{A=1\}`. Seeing all prefix grades does not reveal the receiver response to the untaken continuation action.

**Diagnostic-arm conflict.** If full rollout chooses uniformly among eight classes including three intentionally harmful/privileged diagnostics, a matching stopping policy inherits those diagnostics whenever it continues. It is not in a policy class that forbids those classes. To evaluate a fair stopping-only policy without weights, collect full rollouts under a separately frozen admissible continuation law (for example the declared baseline's allowed mixture), or use genuinely matching existing rollouts. Dropping diagnostic branches does not automatically produce an unweighted sample of full trajectories from that admissible law. It changes selection probabilities and can change history distribution. Stopping immediately is a trivial exception; no continuation is then used.

## 3. What the policy can know: stopping values and the oracle gap

The analyst knows `U_k` for a realized prefix. The deployable rule usually does not. Define

$$
s_k=E[U_k\mid\mathcal F_k^O].
$$

This is the conditional utility of accepting now. Predicting it from public information is a supervised conditional-mean problem. The continuation alternative remains a sequential problem: its value depends on future actions, public information, receiver transitions, and costs.

### Theorem S3: public optimal stopping recursion

Under a fixed continuation law and bounded integrable prefix utilities, define the public Snell recursion

$$
S_D=s_D,\qquad
c_k=E[S_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k^O],\qquad
S_k=\max(s_k,c_k),\quad k<D.
\tag{S3}
$$

Then `E S_0` is the largest achievable expected utility over public stopping times. The first time `s_k\ge c_k` (with mandatory stop at `D`) is optimal.

**Proof.** For any public stopping time, `E U_\tau=\sum_k E[1\{\tau=k\}E(U_k|\mathcal F_k^O)]=E s_\tau`. At `D`, accepting is mandatory. At an earlier prefix, stopping gives `s_k`; continuing and then behaving optimally gives `E[S_{k+1}|\mathcal F_k^O]` by induction. The greater is attainable by an `\mathcal F_k^O`-measurable choice. Backward induction gives both the upper bound and an attaining stopping rule. ∎

**Measurability versus computational access.** If a benchmark and its hidden tests are fixed deterministic functions of the task and artifact, the grade may mathematically be measurable in the sigma-field generated by the full task/artifact even though the deployed program is forbidden to run those tests or cannot compute that function. Removing the grade column alone does not prove a strict sigma-field separation. A formal information model can treat the withheld evaluation assets as latent random baseline variables; an operational implementation can instead restrict the allowed feature computations and policy class explicitly. The Snell optimum assumes knowledge of the conditional law and is a theoretical class optimum, not a claim that a fitted model can compute it. The public/analyst distinction must use the declared interpretation consistently.

This recursion is on the **entire public filtration**, not automatically on an 87-coordinate current feature vector. A feature-state implementation needs conditional value/transition sufficiency, or must be presented as a restricted approximation. Conversely, `\mathcal F_k^O` being strictly smaller than the analyst's information does **not** itself prove insufficiency: excluded hidden variables may be irrelevant to the conditional law of all future public outcomes. The original theory's representation assumptions remain the correct criterion.

### Theorem S4: regret from stopping-advantage error

Let `\Delta_k=c_k-s_k` be the optimal public continuation advantage. A fitted rule `\widehat\tau` continues when `\widehat\Delta_k>0` and stops otherwise. For the same fixed continuation law, define `r_k=|\Delta_k|` if its current stop/continue choice disagrees with the optimal sign, and zero otherwise. Then

$$
V^*-V(\widehat\tau)
=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}E[1\{\widehat\tau\ge k\}r_k]
\le\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}E[1\{\widehat\tau\ge k\}
|\widehat\Delta_k-\Delta_k|].
\tag{S4}
$$

If `|\widehat\Delta_k-\Delta_k|\le\varepsilon_k` almost surely and `P(|\Delta_k|\le x)\le C_kx^{\alpha_k}` for `x>0`, the regret is at most `\sum_k C_k\varepsilon_k^{1+\alpha_k}`.

**Proof.** At a reached decision, the difference between the optimal public value `S_k` and the fitted action evaluated with optimal continuation is zero for an optimal choice and `|\Delta_k|` for the wrong choice. Telescope these one-step Bellman differences along the fitted stopped path; the terminal comparison vanishes, giving the equality. A sign disagreement implies `|\Delta_k|\le|\widehat\Delta_k-\Delta_k|`, proving the first bound. Uniform error confines every mistaken decision to `|\Delta_k|\le\varepsilon_k`; bound its regret by `\varepsilon_k` and apply the margin probability bound. Dropping the reached-event indicator only enlarges the bound. ∎

The theorem estimates the **combined** continuation-minus-stop advantage. Estimating only `P(current artifact correct|public features)` does not identify continuation gains or the best next feedback. The bounds are conditional on error/margin control; they do not assert those conditions hold for the current fitted critic.

### Proposition S5: observable oracle ceilings and unavailable information

For any public stopping rule satisfying S1–S4,

$$
E[U_\tau]\le E[\max_{0\le k\le D}U_k].
\tag{S5}
$$

**Proof.** `U_\tau\le\max_kU_k` for each complete rollout. Integrate. ∎

The right side is a retrospective full-path upper bound, not a deployable rule. Even revealing the **current** hidden grade is extra information; revealing all future grades is additionally anticipatory. A pathwise maximum of net utility is at least as large as every causal stopping value but need not be attainable even by an analyst who sees only current hidden grades. For independent fair binary grades with no informative public signal and zero cost, any allowed rule has mean one half, while the best of two hidden grades has mean three quarters. Thus an oracle gap need not be capturable by any policy in the allowed information set.

The inequality covers only the same complete continuation law and recorded horizon. It is not an upper bound on another prompt policy or on continuing past an observed natural dropout. If a log ended early and the proposed “oracle” imputes a repair rate for missing suffixes, that number is a **model-based extrapolation**; it is not the observed right side of (S5). The reported `+0.046` from the sibling audit explicitly makes such an imputation and must retain that qualifier.

## 4. Failure, time caps, and incomplete observation

If a service error occurs during continuation, define before collection whether it is a transition to an absorbing failure artifact with a specified utility or a missing observation. Under the first choice it is an observed outcome; full-rollout replay stays valid only if the target incurs the same error law and all prefix utilities remain defined. A collected timeout cannot be silently removed because it weakens the policy.

For the second choice, let `R_k` indicate that the complete information needed to evaluate prefix `k` is observed, with monotone missingness. Let `g_s` be the conditional probability of observing the next prefix, given recorded past information and `R_s=1`. Assume sequential coarsening at random: conditional on that past, survival of observation is independent of future complete prefix variables. Assume `g_s>0` wherever the target can continue, and put `G_k=\prod_{s<k}g_s`, `G_0=1`. Scores missing at the initial prefix require their own observation factor.

### Theorem S6: censoring-weighted prefix evaluation

Under the preceding observation assumptions and S1–S3,

$$
V(e,\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^D E\left[
\frac{R_k}{G_k}1\{\tau=k\}U_k\right].
\tag{S6}
$$

The summand is evaluated only when `R_k=1`, in which case its whole required prefix is available.

**Proof.** For any integrable complete-prefix variable `F_k`, iterate conditional expectation over the observation indicators. At each observation decision, the conditional mean of its factor `1\{R_{s+1}=1\}/g_s`, given the complete history relevant to `F_k` and prior observed survival, is one by coarsening at random. Thus `E[R_k F_k/G_k]=E F_k`. Apply this with `F_k=1\{\tau=k\}U_k` and sum. ∎

A different target continuation adds its action ratios from (S2). If a target would continue beyond a deterministic wall-time cap where `g_s=0`, the future value is not identified without further modeling. A full nominal turn budget does not imply complete observation when slow or failing trajectories are truncated. Conditioning on successful completion generally changes the estimand. If a common wall-time deadline itself is part of the policy, define it as an observed intervention/outcome rule with complete utility, and ensure prefix costs/latencies remain transferable under S2.

## 5. Independent resampling and candidate selection are different interventions

Suppose a bank contains candidate outputs `B_1,...,B_K` generated independently conditional on the task by a fixed initial-prompt receiver law. A selector sees allowed candidate information `\mathcal G`, then chooses index `J`; hidden candidate scores `Y_1,...,Y_K` are available to the analyst. When all candidates and grades are observed, a frozen selector's value is `E Y_J` without action weights. This is **full-information selection from a fixed candidate bank**, not identification of the effect of a new feedback message at a changing conversation history.

Independence is useful for modeling resampling but not needed for the unweighted selection identity. What is needed is that deployment produces the same joint bank distribution, allowed signals, and costs. A bank made from different model versions, adaptive routing, or multi-round continuations defines a different selection target. Exchangeability among repeated samples does not turn them into independent task clusters.

### Theorem S7: Bayes selection and the information gap

For selectors measurable in `\mathcal G`, let `m_j=E[Y_j|\mathcal G]`. With finite `K` and measurable tie breaking,

$$
\sup_{J\ \mathcal G\text{-measurable}}E[Y_J]
=E[\max_{1\le j\le K}m_j]
\le E[\max_{1\le j\le K}Y_j].
\tag{S7}
$$

**Proof.** Conditional on `\mathcal G`, any (possibly randomized) selector has mean `\sum_jP(J=j|\mathcal G)m_j`, at most the largest `m_j`; the argmax attains it. For the second inequality, each `m_j\le E[\max_lY_l|\mathcal G]`; maximize and integrate. ∎

An oracle pass-at-K gap is a ceiling, not a demonstrated recoverable gain. A null result for two fitted selectors means those fitted procedures did not demonstrate improvement with the available data. It does **not** prove `E max_j m_j` equals the baseline, prove all learning methods on the same features fail, or establish irreducible insufficiency of that feature map.

A sequential resampler can generate candidates one at a time, stop at a public signal, and return the current candidate; Theorem S1 applies to its own full resampling trajectories. If it can return any earlier stored candidate, define both a stopping time `\tau` and an `\mathcal F_\tau^O`-measurable terminal selector `J\le\tau`; the path functional is `Y_J-\lambda C_\tau`. Identical coupling proves unweighted replay under a matching bank-generation mechanism. If it generates all `K` candidates before selecting, it incurs all `K` generation costs. Retrospectively finding an early successful candidate does not entitle a selector that inspected later candidates to the earlier cost.

To attribute performance to feedback content rather than selection or extra samples, independently compare frozen policies under matched information and total cost. A resampling selector's success establishes no causal feedback benefit; a feedback policy's failure against one resampler is evidence about that tested comparison, not a theorem that no future feedback policy can help.

## 6. What finite-sample guarantees and ESS bounds do—and do not—say

### Theorem S8: honest finite-class unweighted evaluation

Condition on a training sample that fixes `m` candidate stopping/selection rules. Suppose each independent root yields a prespecified averaged score for each rule in `[a,b]`. Let `n` be the number of independent identically sampled root clusters. With probability at least `1-\alpha`, simultaneously,

$$
|\widehat V_j-V_j|\le(b-a)
\sqrt{\frac{\log(2m/\alpha)}{2n}},\quad 1\le j\le m.
\tag{S8}
$$

**Proof.** Each frozen rule's cluster scores are bounded independent draws. Hoeffding's inequality controls its two tails, and a union bound over `m` rules proves (S8). Within-cluster averaging preserves the range regardless of dependence within the root. ∎

For paired rule differences in `[-B,B]`, substitute range `2B`, or use a separately justified variance-sensitive bound. Such worst-case intervals can be too broad to resolve a small effect. This establishes the limitations of **that bound**, not impossibility of every finite-sample method. As an explicit counterexample, take a paired difference `D_i\in\{-1,+1\}` and suppose all `n` independent observations are `+1`. Under the null `E D_i\le0`, the success probability is at most one half, so the event has probability at most `2^{-n}`. The test rejecting only on that event is valid and can certify a positive mean at `n=176` (indeed much smaller), although a conservative multirule worst-case radius could be uninformative for the particular project gap. This example is not a claim that the project's observed differences have that distribution or evidence.

A finite-sample paired bootstrap interval is not automatically a nonasymptotic certificate. Its validity needs its own cluster-sampling, regularity, and estimator conditions. Evaluation of an independently frozen rule is a regular mean problem; estimating the **optimal** value after searching unrestricted rules can be nonregular near tied action values. Binary success probabilities near zero or one alone do not imply a tied action contrast or identify the mass of an exceptional law.

### Proposition S9: overlap bounds are lower bounds on an idealized ESS

Suppose nonnegative stopped weights satisfy `E W=1` and `W\le M`. Then `E W^2\le M`, so the population proxy

$$
n_{\rm eff}^{\rm pop}=n\frac{(E W)^2}{E W^2}
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad n_{\rm eff}^{\rm pop}\ge n/M.
\tag{S9}
$$

**Proof.** `W^2\le MW` pointwise; take expectations and rearrange. ∎

For eight equally randomized actions and two deterministic overrides, the **worst-case** path weight is 64, so `176/64=2.75` is a lower bound under the proxy, not the actual number of effective clusters. For example, a target only slightly different from the logging selector at both decisions has maximum bound 64 but second moment close to one, hence ESS close to 176. The empirical ratio `(\sum_iW_i)^2/\sum_iW_i^2` is random and is not guaranteed by the population bound to exceed `n/M` in every realized sample. Cluster averaging can alter the appropriate weights/variance calculation. Actual precision depends on scores, outcomes, cluster dependence, and covariance—not just the weight proxy.

Likewise a sufficient planning expression such as `n\ge8M\log(4m/\alpha)/gap^2` is not an information-theoretic necessary sample size unless accompanied by a matching lower-bound theorem for the exact experiment and target. Clipping a weighted utility has sign-definite bias only under the stated utility sign restriction. A raw utility `Y-\lambda C` can be negative; with `Y\in[0,1]` and `C\in[0,C_{max}]` its range length is `1+\lambda C_{max}`, not one unless explicitly normalized.

### Proposition S10: hidden carried state does not automatically defeat randomized policy evaluation

Let `L_k` be unrecorded internal generator/receiver state. Suppose logging actions are genuinely randomized with known `e_k(a|H_k)` independent of `L_k` conditional on the full recorded history, the target actions use `\pi_k(a|H_k)`, and both executions share the same initial latent-state distribution and state-transition kernel `K(dH_{k+1},dL_{k+1}|H_k,L_k,A_k)`. Assume support. Then the joint latent-and-observed trajectory likelihood ratio is the observable product `\prod_k\pi_k/e_k`, and the same ratio identifies the marginal observed trajectory law after integrating out `L`.

**Proof.** Factor the augmented trajectory densities. The common initial law and every common latent/environment transition cancel, leaving only the action-probability ratios. This remaining product is measurable in the observed trajectory. Therefore, when marginalizing the latent path, it can be taken outside the integral and remains the marginal density ratio. ∎

Thus the blanket claim that any carried cache, scratchpad, or action-dependent hidden memory necessarily prevents cancellation is too strong. Hidden memory creates real dangers when it affects assignment beyond logged propensities, changes mechanism across collection/deployment, is omitted from a history needed to reconstruct assignment, causes cross-root interference, or prevents faithful branch restoration. It can also obstruct a reduced-state transition model. Stateless generation is a useful sufficient engineering safeguard; byte-identical output under a stochastic generator is neither necessary for this theorem nor a general test of distributional equality.

## 7. Decision for Q11 and the next bounded experiment

**Q11 resolution:** the primary methodological claim should be **identification and honest evaluation of supported language-intervention regimes**, with horizon-aware stopping/selection as a concrete application. The current evidence does not support a headline that a new causal critic improves decisions over correctly history-adjusted sequential regression. Neither does the selection pilot yet establish a learned-selection improvement. This is a scope decision, not a declaration of empirical success or novelty priority.

The narrower immediately testable target is the out-of-task value of a frozen, public-information stopping/selection policy against a strong matched-cost baseline. If full rollouts match its continuation, evaluate by paired unweighted root means. If it changes feedback content, generation, or receiver routing, use a supported action-value design or fresh paired execution; do not label that comparison `W=1` merely because STOP grades are observed.

The original goal of learning next-prompt values remains legitimate as a separate experiment: randomize supported candidate prompts from restored prefixes and evaluate specified continuations. It should not borrow evidence from independent-sample selection. A scope-limited negative result is an acceptable outcome. A claim that a whole feature family, all feedback policies, or all certificates are impossible requires substantially stronger analysis than the current pilot and worst-case inequalities.
