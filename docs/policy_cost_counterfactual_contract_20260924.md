# LEAD-POLICY-03: distinguish trial expenditure from policy deployment cost

Coordinating scientific lead, 24 September 2026. **Prospective cost-contract correction, not a new cost result or trial release.** This repairs a fairness ambiguity in our own [policy design](independent_prompt_policy_validation_design_20260922.md) and [comparator audit](policy_comparator_contract_audit_20260923.md). Their instruction to give all policies the same public diagnostic is correct for a landmark *quality* comparison, but charging that check to every end-to-end policy can be wrong. Information availability in a trial does not force a comparator to run a check it would never use.

Keep three ledgers, preserving every observed call, token, elapsed time, executor start and paid dollar. **Research expenditure** counts the initial receiver call, the diagnostic constructed for each study checkpoint, all executed branches, grading, controls and failures. It is never divided by or credited to a hypothetical policy after the fact. **Landmark incremental deployment cost** starts after the frozen public history `X` exists, so the initial call and diagnostic are sunk for every policy; count only subsequent selector work, continuation/redraws, public checks and acceptance. **End-to-end deployment cost** starts at the public task: count the initial call once and then only the diagnostic, selector, continuation, redraw and post-check operations required by that frozen policy. Common work is charged once when actually required, not mechanically charged to all policies because the trial measured it.

For the primary same-prefix `d` versus fixed `b1` quality contrast, both choose a continuation from the diagnostic-enabled checkpoint; their end-to-end paths require that diagnostic. For a separately frozen bare-task independent-sampling controller `b2`, the trial can still construct `X` for paired quality evaluation and give it to `b2`, which may ignore it. If `b2` is deployed on the **unconditional task population** and its exact algorithm never uses the initial diagnostic, its end-to-end ledger omits that check. If the study instead restricts eligibility to, for example, public-fail histories and deployment must identify eligible tasks, then the eligibility diagnostic belongs in `b2`'s end-to-end ledger too. These are distinct target/operational contracts: declare sampling and eligibility before any evaluation outcome. No policy may access a private grade or another branch, and no policy is forced to waste its allowance or quietly use uncharged information.

The **study** recorded fourteen shared public-diagnostic starts; its hypothetical per-policy accounting assigned zero such starts to N0/STOP and one per root to N1/S1/R1. These are development bookkeeping examples, not estimates of a future policy's total expense. A one-call ceiling or equal research expenditure does not establish equal tokens, latency, energy or end-to-end cost. Freeze one common numerical *allowance* from a stated starting line; report realized resources under each algorithm and quality inference separately. A formal savings or joint quality-and-cost claim needs its own prespecified estimand, failure handling and multiplicity rule.
