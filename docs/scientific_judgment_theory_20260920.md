# Scientific judgment on our original adaptive-prompting design

**Date:** 2026-09-20. **Authorial responsibility:** this audits the theory workstream's own research choices. The experimental agents implemented and explored a program we helped set. Mathematical corrections are necessary, but pointing to implementation mistakes does not discharge our responsibility for choosing the question, sequence of experiments, comparators, and stopping rules.

**Sources reviewed:** our original `docs/research_proposal.md`, `docs/theory.md`, `docs/experiment_protocol.md`, `docs/training_and_serving.md`, and `docs/experiment_handoff.md`; the initial proposal/protocol/handoff were also reopened from commit `cf2e65c` to avoid crediting later corrections to the original plan. Current negative/positive findings are taken only at their audited scope. This is a scientific judgment memo, not a new literature review or proof expansion.

## 1. Judgment

The original question is worthwhile but not yet empirically supported: **can a public-history model choose the next feedback prompt better than a competent simple policy, for a frozen receiver and a named continuation?** We have not completed the smallest prospective experiment that answers it. The existing theory and simulations establish how to identify and estimate a value under conditions; they do not establish that useful prompt heterogeneity exists, that it is predictable from permitted information, or that exploiting it pays for the additional computation.

Our original documents did explicitly require correctly history-adjusted regression, separate inference from prediction, and disclaim neural optimality and real-model superiority. Those safeguards were sound. They were not enough. We still laid out a five-stage path from critic training to a learned generator before requiring evidence that the first decision bottleneck existed. The handoff made P3 conditional on a successful leakage/identification audit, rather than on demonstrated decision value. That was our design error.

The right current decision is to **pause generator training, architecture expansion, and more generic theorem development**. Complete a small, fresh, same-target prompt-decision study if its prespecified precision is affordable. If it is not affordable, retain a methods/design package and report empirical uncertainty; do not substitute a different easier result and call the original idea validated.

## 2. The assumptions we allowed to become a research trajectory

| Our original choice | What was too strong or insufficiently tested | Responsibility and correction |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal: history encoder → generator → causal critic → adaptive selector | This architecture assumed several useful links: candidates differ in downstream value; useful ordering varies across histories; permitted features predict that ordering; a learned selector captures it; gains survive costs. Identification proves none of these. | We should have required a measured bottleneck test before specifying the whole learned system. Test incremental prompt selection first. |
| Protocol S0–S1: broad robustness grid before substantive task evidence | A large synthetic grid can repeatedly confirm established identities without showing a real decision problem that benefits from the method. Our many axes diluted the main comparison and made completion expensive. | Use a small diagnostic grid to validate the exact target and estimator, then move to the bottleneck experiment. Additional scenarios need a specific unresolved question. |
| Protocol S2: matched DR and regression critics | We correctly named the strong baseline, but did not state a falsifiable benefit expected specifically from DR in a randomized known-propensity design. A theoretically robust estimator may have higher finite-sample label variance and no ranking gain. | Treat DR versus adjusted regression as a comparative method question, not an expected victory. Either can be the better decision tool. |
| Protocol S3: three feedback decisions, generated candidates, STOP, later broader slates | Several mechanisms changed together before knowing whether next-prompt content mattered. A gain could come from more samples, stopping, an informative checker, or prompt content. A null could come from any of them. | First isolate one personalized prompt choice under a fixed future continuation and a common stopping schedule. Add adaptive stopping only after interpreting that result. |
| Proposal: pilot to avoid floor/ceiling tasks | A pilot-based application choice is reasonable, but we did not clearly separate mechanical suitability from outcome-driven selection of a benchmark favorable to our method. Moderate accuracy is not evidence of action heterogeneity or observability. | Declare the initial target population before comparison. Changing difficulty distribution later is a new population study, with its own hypothesis. |
| Protocol S4: many baselines and a quality–cost frontier | “Strong baseline” was too open. Independent resampling with a cheap public check was not made a named required practical comparator. Frontier language can conceal an unmatched compute advantage. | Separate a prompt-selection mechanism comparison from an eventual deployment comparison against a frozen resampling/stop baseline with measured full costs. |
| Original repeated-prefix study: 80 prefixes × 4 candidates × 4 continuations | Four replicates do not supply precise individual conditional truth for small effects. We budgeted an impressive branch count before calculating the error of the branch means or how many independent roots remained. | Use branch outcomes for pooled/calibrated diagnostics with their sampling error, not to certify exact-history blips. Base decisive sample size on independent-root paired policy differences. |
| Training specification: rank by net Q, then advantage-weighted generator training | Ranking noisy candidate values creates selection bias; distillation may amplify it. The warning existed, but the workflow still advanced without a usefulness gate. | A generator update is not needed to test the original next-prompt question. Require independent supported-selection benefit before training the generator. |
| Handoff completion criteria | Several gates described artifacts, tests, valid logging, and result reporting rather than a scientific decision to continue or stop. | We own the go/no-go criteria and must specify them before new test outcomes. More valid files are not evidence of a useful mechanism. |

The discrepancy between candidate-first slate randomization in our theory and class-first generation in subsequent experiments also reflects an incomplete design freeze that we should have resolved before collection. Both designs can be valid; leaving the choice open allowed the scientific object to drift.

## 3. Three questions that must stay separate

**Identification:** under the declared intervention, support, and consistency assumptions, does the observed law determine a conditional mean or policy value? Randomization and exact likelihood accounting answer this. A negative practical result does not falsify an identification theorem. Conversely, proving identification does not make the effect large or estimable at the available sample size.

**Ranking/personalization:** does useful prompt ordering vary with public history, and does the fitted model predict that variation out of sample? Uniformly effective prompts can improve average performance without requiring personalization. Large marginal differences between classes can coexist with almost no gain from a history-specific selector. Conditional mean prediction can improve while decision ranking stays unchanged.

**Policy benefit:** does the frozen selector improve final outcomes enough to justify its generation, validation, and inference cost against a competent alternative? Correct ranking may still deliver negligible gains if candidates are similar, the baseline already captures the opportunity, or extra calls dominate the gain. This is the practical endpoint the original program ultimately promised to investigate.

We should not use a success at an earlier link to claim a later link. Nor should we insist that a negative result at a later link means the entire causal formulation was false.

## 4. What the current evidence actually rejects

1. **The broad “causal correction is needed for good decisions” motivation was too strong.** The state-conditioned simulation results and corrected comparisons support this criticism. Correctly adjusted sequential regression is already a causal estimator under the required assumptions. The results do not prove that DR can never help under specified nuisance error, overlap, or data limitations.
2. **The tested simple iterative loop is not an automatically competitive use of calls.** Resampling is a serious comparator. The corrected routing evidence varies by model, sample count, and cost; it is not the promised fixed-receiver randomized next-prompt experiment and cannot reject every feedback policy.
3. **The tested small-bank agreement procedures did not demonstrate the proposed benefit at their measured costs.** That is a meaningful negative for those procedures. It is not an impossibility theorem about all publicly informed selectors, and its budget differs from the four-call fixed-bank logistic pilot.
4. **Cheap features and a visible check have not produced a stable general solution.** The old null pilots remain negative for the procedures and cohorts tested. The corrected 7B logistic signal is an exploratory exception worth retaining honestly; it does not resolve the original prompting question or establish a cheaper sequential controller.
5. **We have not measured the original decision bottleneck directly.** There is still no decisive new-task study that holds the original request, receiver, available information, candidate mechanism, and continuation fixed while asking whether learned selection of the next feedback beats a competent same-target comparator.

These conclusions are enough to cut scope. They are not a reason to search indefinitely for a benchmark, feature set, or significance threshold that makes the original narrative succeed.

## 5. Repair versus change of question

| Action we have proposed or taken | Same-target repair? | Scientific treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Correct an omitted propensity, continuation target, truth calculation, or post-routing exclusion | Yes, if it restores the originally stated target | Preserve failed output; rerun the declared target; distinguish numerical correction from new evidence. |
| Add the originally required same-capacity history-aware regression baseline | Yes | This is overdue fulfillment of our initial design, not scope expansion. |
| Separate analyst labels from public serving inputs; count previously omitted cost | Yes | Restore the declared information/cost contract. A repaired effect may shrink or disappear. |
| Replace natural early-stop logs with genuinely complete rollouts for a stopping target | New data/design, same stopping estimand only if continuation and target are preserved | Necessary collection, not recovery of unobserved suffixes by relabeling logs. |
| Move from next-prompt intervention to choosing among four initial answers | No | A separate fixed-bank selection study. Keep the promising 7B result, but do not call it confirmation of personalized feedback. |
| Move from prompt content selection to STOP/continue under unchanged prompts | Restriction to a different component | Useful diagnostic/substudy; not completion of the original language-treatment objective. |
| Switch to moderately difficult tasks after observing ceiling effects or null gains | Population change | New target population and prospective test; no retrospective rescue of the old target. |
| Increase candidate count or use a stronger/different receiver | Intervention/compute or environment change | State the new bank/model law and cost; do not combine effect sizes as one experiment. |
| Add generated probe tests, execution agreement, or external evidence | Information and often treatment/budget change | May be a good new system, but isolate the added-information contribution and use a correspondingly strong baseline. |
| Train a new prompt generator after a negative fixed-generator result | Intervention-distribution change | An exploratory extension, not a statistical repair. It needs fresh data and cannot be justified solely by critic-predicted improvement. |

Some changes may ultimately be the best research direction. Our responsibility is to say that we changed the question, explain why, and preserve the negative answer to the old one.

## 6. Minimum decisive same-target experiment

The next experiment should be a **landmark prompt-selection trial**, not the entire autonomous-user system. This deliberately restricts the original multiround target to its necessary first link; passing it would justify, not replace, a later full-policy study.

**Freeze before new evaluation outcomes:** one receiver digest; one task population/family split; the original task and first-answer protocol; one frozen candidate generator; exact rendering and public-information permissions; a continuation policy; outcome scorer; failure rules; total token/call limits; and the candidate policies. Use a new root/family-separated evaluation set. Previously inspected benchmark labels are development data, not confirmatory data.

**Intervention and horizon:** after the common initial answer, form the same small candidate slate at the same decision point. Keep the original simple candidates—generic retry, verification, and a task-specific prompt from the frozen proposer. Compare next-prompt choices while **holding the stop schedule and future continuation fixed**; for example, one selected feedback followed by one fixed verification continuation. The initial task is unchanged, and hidden evaluator outputs are unavailable to the policy. This tests a continuation-specific prompt value, not the effect of a new task, stronger model, or different number of turns.

STOP may be recorded as a secondary reference using the current artifact's score, but the primary content-selection comparison should not allow one arm to win simply by adopting a better stopping rule. If the implementation cannot preserve that distinction, split the two mechanisms into named contrasts rather than attributing the combined gain to prompting.

**Training:** collect known-probability choices among those exact candidates on training roots. Fit two matched models: sequential outcome regression and DR-label regression, using the same public inputs, capacity, training data, and tuning budget. A predeclared development criterion chooses one final learned selector. Freeze it before evaluating. A simple fixed/heuristic comparator is selected from a predeclared short list on the same development roots and then frozen. This avoids forcing the more elaborate estimator to be the winner.

**Primary estimand:** the independent-root paired difference in final success between the frozen learned next-prompt selector and that competent fixed/heuristic selector, with the same history distribution and continuation. It directly asks whether personalization of prompt choice adds value. Execute both branches from faithfully restored common prefixes, with independent receiver randomness or a declared common-random-number coupling; neither method sees the other's response or hidden score. Record all calls, failures, and outcomes. A small repeated-branch subset supports calibration diagnostics, but the policy contrast is primary.

**Method-specific secondary contrast:** DR selector versus matched regression selector under the identical intervention and continuation. If this contrast is promoted to a second primary claim, account for multiplicity before collection. Lack of DR superiority does not invalidate a useful regression-based prompt policy; it does invalidate a claim that DR is the source of the practical improvement in that experiment.

**Practical comparator:** the mechanism trial charges the common candidate-generation protocol to both arms to isolate selection. That does not prove deployment efficiency, because a fixed baseline could skip the proposer. Therefore any advancement claim also reports actual deployable costs and a frozen independent-resampling/public-check comparator under a separate, explicit common budget. Do not retrospectively give the resampler extra samples or subtract unused generator costs to make a preferred ordering. If this comparison is not run, restrict the result to the prompt-selection mechanism.

This is the smallest experiment that can decide whether there is a useful **learnable next-prompt choice** in the intended regime. It does not yet establish general free-form generation, arbitrary-history effect inference, human personalization, or joint optimal stopping.

## 7. Acceptance and stopping rules—prospective, not invented after the result

No rule can make the analyses already performed preregistered. The following are **new recommended decision rules**, to be committed before fresh evaluation. They are not claims that the original protocol already contained them.

1. **Freeze the scientific scale.** Retain the original protocol's five-percentage-point planning contrast (`δ*=0.05`) as the default target for a useful prompt-selection gain unless the design owner specifies another practically justified threshold before new data. It was an original planning illustration, not a previously binding acceptance threshold. Do not tune it to the observed +2.85-point selection pilot, which is another target. Smaller effects can be scientifically real; they simply do not trigger this default advancement gate.
2. **Choose sample size by the paired-root variance, not branch count.** Use training/development or a separate feasibility batch to estimate the contrast variance and root dependence. Lock the sample size and attainable interval width before opening final outcomes. The original approximation is `n≈(z_(1−α/2)+z_power)^2 Var(D)/δ*²`, with the declared multiplicity and clustering adjustment. If the required new independent roots or calls exceed the agreed budget, stop at feasibility and report the resulting uncertainty; do not substitute seeds for roots or quietly lower δ*.
3. **Separate three possible final outcomes.** Advance to a larger dynamic-policy study only if the prespecified 95% interval excludes zero in favor of the learned selector, the point estimate is at least δ*, and the declared cost constraint is met. This is evidence of positive gain with a target-size estimate, **not proof that the true gain exceeds δ***. Stop this method/configuration for practical futility if the interval's upper endpoint is below δ*. If the interval spans δ* and zero or remains otherwise unresolved, report inconclusive and allow only the one prespecified sample-size extension, if any; no repeated threshold or population changes.
4. **A stronger material-benefit claim needs stronger evidence.** To claim that the true improvement itself exceeds δ*, require its lower confidence limit to exceed δ*. Do not equate “significant versus zero” with that claim.
5. **Mechanism-specific failure determines the next action.** If learned and fixed prompting are indistinguishable with a narrow interval, stop personalization for that candidate/receiver/population rather than training a larger generator. If both beat STOP but not resampling at matched cost, retain an effect of continued computation and reject the claimed efficient prompting advantage. If adjusted regression matches or beats DR, keep regression and drop DR-superiority language. If an oracle branch diagnostic is high but the frozen learner fails, report an estimation/information bottleneck; that alone does not license unlimited feature search.
6. **Integrity is a separate gate.** Leakage, mismatched continuation, invalid grader inputs, unrecorded failures, or failed state restoration blocks an efficacy interpretation. Repair and rerun on fresh independent evaluation data where necessary. Do not classify an uninterpretable run as either positive or negative efficacy evidence.
7. **The budget constraint must be numeric before launch.** A statement such as “same cost” is insufficient. Choose one primary resource budget (for example total generation tokens under fixed caps), count initial/generator/receiver/selector/probe calls consistently, and separately report other costs. If a threshold such as a 10% overhead allowance is chosen, identify it as a design choice fixed before evaluation—not a tolerance introduced to save a positive result.

These rules create a genuine chance for the original idea to fail. That is what the initial architecture-first handoff lacked.

## 8. What we should say now

We designed a technically defensible framework but advanced toward a large learned system before establishing its practical premise. The negative experiments have usefully weakened the claim that causal correction itself improves decisions and shown that cheap resampling/stopping is a demanding baseline. They have not yet answered the original randomized next-prompt question. Some of our later “corrections” are real statistical repairs; others are new research targets and must be labeled that way.

The scientific priority is one affordable, frozen, same-target prompt-choice test with the possibility of a clear negative conclusion. If it cannot be run with adequate precision, the honest endpoint is a scoped methods package and unresolved empirical utility—not another round of proofs, a change of benchmark until something wins, or blame assigned to the agents who executed the program.
