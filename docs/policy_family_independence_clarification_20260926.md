## Three separate claims

1. **Eligibility and separation.** Source identity, specification agreement, endpoint discrimination, prior exposure and semantic relationships determine whether a task/family belongs in a declared development or untouched evaluation roster. Similarity scores retrieve evidence; human grouping is a documented design judgment. Neither supplies a probabilistic independence certificate. Keep related roots together under the final frozen grouping and retain every exclusion/hold.
2. **Stochastic independence for an interval.** The [accepted bounded-mean contract](paired_kl_inference_contract_20260926.md) requires independent complete family contrast vectors conditional on frozen development information. A valid random-input/sampling law and stable isolated execution must justify that condition. Distinct IDs, different seed labels, chronological execution or successful containment canaries alone do not. A random common server state or data-dependent adaptation may induce dependence even for semantically different tasks.
3. **Population interpretation.** Even independent observations do not identify the value on an arbitrary population. The target population, sampling law, within-family weights, source/exposure restrictions and any transport assumptions must connect the average expectation in the interval to the claimed deployment population. A source-selected convenience frame does not acquire representative sampling by receiving new seeds.

## Conditional fixed-roster fact and its limit

Let F contain the frozen development information, selected policies, task roster, endpoint and execution protocol. Suppose each root's complete paired contrast is D_i=f_i(U_i;F), bounded in [-1,1], with mutually independent complete random inputs U_i conditional on F. Then the D_i are conditionally independent, even if two fixed task descriptions are semantically related or identical. This follows from closure of conditional independence under measurable coordinatewise transformations, not a new theorem. Applying the classical bounded-mean argument would target the average of E[D_i|F] over that fixed roster. Conditioning on F is legitimate only when the specified product law remains valid under that conditioning; the roster/policy must not be selected using these evaluation draws.

The complete random inputs include initial generation, diagnostics, continuations and any stochastic scoring/environment components. Correct conditional action marginals, branch restoration and the predeclared coupling remain required. A common execution order may be included in F only if the resulting conditional laws satisfy the claim. A cap or failure process may mask latent complete outcomes; it does not erase assigned roots or restore invalid latent-law assumptions. The existing pathwise missingness envelope remains applicable only under its original containment assumptions.

This observation **does not authorize** treating related roots as new untouched families, counting repeated seeds as independent tasks, changing equal-family weighting to root pooling, shrinking the family unit after seeing data, reanalyzing old results, or substituting a fixed-roster success for the original population objective. The currently proposed inference API, alpha allocation, family aggregation, endpoint laws and collection hold remain unchanged. If a later explicitly labeled finite-roster measurement study is useful, report that distinct target and its limitations; it earns no untouched-family policy-validation credit.
