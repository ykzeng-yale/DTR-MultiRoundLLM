1. **Removing one checkpoint changes the target.** Dropping mbpp/863 moves the four-checkpoint mean to
   **+1/24 = +0.042**. That is a **different quantity, not a corrected estimate of the declared one**: the
   declared target is the equally weighted contrast at exactly these five fixed checkpoints, and the
   −0.100 stands for it. Six draws do not establish the sign of any checkpoint's response-mean difference,
   so the leave-one-out figure is reported as dependence on one checkpoint, not as a rival estimate.
2. **The arms differ in recorded output validity, not only in answers.** Under the frozen rule, R1's 13
   zero-scoring invalid outputs are **one extraction rejection plus 12 Python-AST failures**, against **0 + 0**
   for FRESH. They are not 13 extractor rejections. All six R1 outputs at mbpp/966 fail to produce parseable
   Python, and the saved failures **include diagnostic-report echoes** — output that repeats the public
   diagnostic report instead of emitting a program. They are **not shown to be correct solutions rejected
   cosmetically**, and no latent semantic success is recovered from them. The frozen endpoint is the
   private-suite-**plus-output-validity** score, so these are legitimate zeros. I deliberately did **not**
   compute a pass rate among parseable outputs only: that conditions on a property realised after the
   continuation, which is post-treatment selection and not a causal contrast.

**Discipline checks, reported because a negative reading needs them.**

- **The saved score is not constant on all outputs:** both outcomes 0 and 1 occur, and the largest
  single-checkpoint movement is 0.667. Ten cells produced more than one distinct output **text**, but textual
  distinctness is **not** ten varying binary-outcome cells and does **not** establish a discriminating semantic
  instrument; none is claimed.
- **Observed saturation, not structural limits:** mbpp/863 FRESH scored 1 on all six observed draws; mbpp/652
  scored 0 on all six in **both** arms; mbpp/966 R1 scored 0 on all six. These are **observed** over six draws.
  No floor or ceiling is structural, and **652's observed tie does not imply it can never express a difference**.
- **No post-treatment selection:** 60 assigned, 60 graded, 0 dropped after the continuation.

- **Observed differences, per checkpoint, with no conditional sign claimed.** 842 +0.500, 288 −0.167,
  863 −0.667, 966 −0.167, 652 0.000. No conditional sign and no reliable ranking of checkpoints follows from
  six draws each. An equally weighted mean of five fixed checkpoints is not a population effect.
- **This does not identify a mechanism.** R1 and FRESH differ in two ways at once — the diagnostic bytes and
  the instruction — so neither the negative mean nor the per-root differences attribute anything to the
  diagnostic alone. That was true when a tie was the expected outcome and it stays true now that it is not.
- **It is not a futility finding** about the rule, the arm or the project, it does not establish absence of
  learnable heterogeneity, and it does not generalize beyond these five histories.

**Observation on mbpp/863, recorded without a causal claim.** FRESH passed 6/6 where R1 passed 2/6. The lead's
E12 judgment established that 863's initial program resets its run length at duplicate values, returning 2
where the public example requires 3, and that its private suite passes only because those private cases contain
no duplicates. R1 carries that public diagnostic; FRESH does not. Whether carrying it is what moved the private
score here is **not** established by this design, and this run cannot settle it.

**The endpoint remains thin private tests.** 863's known public defect is missed by its own private assertions,
so **a passing score is not complete semantic correctness** — including for the six FRESH passes at 863. This
limits what any score on this suite means, in both arms and in both directions. E12's original grades and
interpretation are preserved.
