# Audit of the newly arrived selection decision

Date: 2026-09-20. Reviewed read-only from `origin/main` commit `0ee7666`: `docs/selection_decision.md`, `experiments/audits/g1b_execution_agreement.py`, and `experiments/audits/g1c_allocation_policy.py`. Line references below refer to that version. No experiment was rerun, and no source or archived result was changed for this review.

**Conclusion:** the new experiments are additional exploratory results for particular candidate-bank selectors, with material data-selection and implementation issues. They do not establish the document's broad claim that the research question is impossible on this regime or that the critic, prompt interventions, and generative policy should be abandoned. A discretionary budget pause could still be reasonable; it must be presented as a project decision rather than a proved impossibility result.

## Blocking issues

1. **Both scripts inherit future-outcome selection.** G1b lines 210–221 and G1c lines 102–113 discard all episodes that later switch receivers, then require three or four retained candidates. They also omit empty generated code. G1b lines 225–233 and G1c lines 116–123 restrict further to tasks admitting usable probes. Consequently these candidate banks are selected using later routing/response information and failure/probe availability. They are not the four original draws per task/receiver. The previously executed raw-data audit found first-candidate success .698 versus .105 for retained versus excluded 3B episodes, demonstrating the magnitude of the inherited selection. The new cluster/headroom fractions and costs cannot be generalized to the complete corpus without correcting this construction. See [the full recheck](experiment_recheck_20260920.md).

2. **The visible-check flag still differs from the executed stopping rule.** G1b line 219 and G1c line 111 use `n_fail==0`. The source receiver loop stops on `validation.passed`. The audit found six first decisions where these differ, including zero-assertion failures. Use the actual flag and retain failures with prespecified outcomes. The new selection decision's “same cheap check” claim is currently inaccurate.

3. **Probe publicness is not established relative to the source outcome.** G1b lines 128–135 construct probes using `split_assertions(task)`, not the source routing experiment's frozen visible-test artifact. In `experiments/common/scoring.py` lines 125–141, HumanEval's newly designated visible assertion is taken from its benchmark `check` function after removing prompt-revealed assertions; lines 115–121 similarly use MBPP's first test. This is a new visibility convention. It does not retroactively remove those assertions from the sibling log's already stored `success_first_candidate` outcome. The probe base retains their inputs, and G1b line 159 also inserts the benchmark check's preamble into candidate execution. No direct expected-answer or hidden-outcome label was found in the selection rule, but consuming grader-derived inputs is not equivalent to using only the source protocol's permitted public inputs. “Nothing touches a graded assertion” is therefore unsupported. Before such a claim, use public-only probe provenance and an outcome explicitly excluding exposed inputs, or label the information intervention and shared grading boundary honestly.

4. **G1c implements a different escalation picker than described.** Its documented policy says escalation returns the agreement-plus-visible pick. G1b's actual agree-plus-visible implementation first restricts to visible-passing indices and clusters within them (lines 267–268). G1c lines 76–79 instead computes the largest cluster over **all** candidates and uses visible pass only to break ties within that cluster. Example: one visible-passing candidate and a larger cluster of visible-failing candidates cause the two policies to return different artifacts. Thus the reported gate is not the declared combined picker. This must be corrected or renamed before interpreting its failure as failure of the specified policy.

5. **Closest cost is not matched cost.** G1c lines 159–172 chooses the uniform policy with nearest observed average draw count. It neither constructs equal-cost mixtures nor requires the distance to be small. The reported gaps of 1.2–1.5 draws are substantial, and only generation counts are charged: sandbox probes, validation, tokens, and latency are absent from the policy-cost comparison. Zero new GPU spend is accurate for reanalysis, but does not make deployment execution free. The gate can be unattractive at its reported extra cost without this being an equal-cost experiment or a proof that every gate is unattractive.

## Overinterpretations in the decision

- **The number 33 is an observed headroom-event count, not the independent sample size.** `selection_decision.md` lines 75–76 and 105–109 replace 448 root tasks with the 33 tasks on which the incumbent failed despite an available successful candidate. Those are outcome-selected positive-opportunity events. Other tasks still inform outcome rates, harmful switches, calibration, and cost. This decomposition does not prove that no affordable study can distinguish two public selectors, nor that a null interval is inevitable.
- **The residual ceiling is counted twice.** The .0737 oracle-minus-visible gap is already the remaining 33/448 headroom after the incumbent's successes. Saying that two-thirds of this .0737 has already been taken (lines 107–109) subtracts the incumbent's contribution a second time. A ceiling is an upper bound on gain under that bank distribution, not a proof the attainable gain is zero.
- **The ceiling has a restricted target.** For a fixed bank, `E[max_j Y_j - Y_visible]` is a valid retrospective upper bound on improvement in outcome over the visible picker. A fitted beta-binomial mixed-bank probability additionally depends on iid draws and the assumed difficulty law; residual incumbent performance requires more than that marginal difficulty fit. Neither quantity bounds a new prompt policy that changes the candidate distribution, a different bank size, or a sequential continuation that generates artifacts unavailable in the bank. Lines 125–127 cannot use this bound to rule out all prompt interventions or the generative actor.
- **These are not five independent replications.** They reuse the same selected routing corpus, labels, and evolving research question. Multiple related analyses can be useful, but their agreement is not independent confirmation. Both null confidence intervals quoted for agreement allow positive gains. Failure to reject zero does not prove zero effect, universal feature insufficiency, or a complete mechanism explaining why all procedures must fail.
- **Old E0/G0e claims do not survive unchanged.** Lines 117–123 repeat the continuation-target and cohort/cost claims corrected in [the independent recheck](experiment_recheck_20260920.md). They cannot serve as unaffected supporting pillars for a final STOP verdict.

## Additional implementation limits

G1b truncates output representations to 200 characters and groups errors by exception class (lines 163–168); absent outputs become the identical `MISSING` vector (line 188). Thus agreement can reflect invalid probes, execution failures, or output collisions. Literal mutation does not guarantee that a probe lies within the task's valid input domain. These are properties of this particular feature construction, not of execution-agreement methods generally. The scripts do not preserve the raw per-candidate probe vectors and full failure diagnostics needed to separate these mechanisms after the run. A claim that there is little useful disagreement requires those diagnostics, not only the cluster count.

The source includes previously designated training and confirmatory tasks, and these exploratory analyses do not reinstate a pristine holdout. G1c additionally chooses its comparison policy from observed costs; the unadjusted intervals are not a prospectively fixed, multiplicity-controlled release certificate. Benchmark families may create dependence beyond task IDs.

## Relation to the corrected fixed-bank selector result

The integrating agent's separate corrected four-candidate-bank diagnostic uses all initial candidates, the actual public check flag, and a frozen root-level train/evaluation split. Any positive 7B logistic-selector finding there concerns a **four-call fixed-bank target**. It is evidence against a blanket assertion that no tested public-feature selector can improve a fixed-bank incumbent, subject to that diagnostic's stated exploratory limits. It does not establish superiority at the roughly 1.1-call cost of an early-stopping resampler, refute every particular gate's negative result, or demonstrate benefits from multi-round prompt feedback. These targets must remain separate.

## Recommended disposition

Preserve the new results as exploratory selected-cohort analyses with a prominent audit link. Refrain from treating their “STOP” conclusion as a scientific gate until candidate eligibility, actual checker semantics, probe provenance, the implemented escalation picker, and cost targets are reconciled. Then predeclare a narrow decision: whether a specific public policy yields enough incremental utility at a specified budget to justify the next experiment. None of this review proves that a larger neural critic or prompt generator will help; it establishes that the present negative claims exceed the evidence.
