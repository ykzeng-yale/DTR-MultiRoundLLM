# Later independent prompt-policy validation: design proposal

22 September 2026. **Prospective design proposal with independent internal review; not a numerical freeze or release of training, simulation or collection.** E12 retains its [fixed-roster descriptive contract](e12_bundled_release_20260922.md); this memo neither replaces it nor applies population inference to its 14 roots. The [current paper](../manuscript/current_paper_20260922.md) and [scientific judgment](scientific_judgment_20260920.md) distinguish identification, predictable prompt choice and independently validated policy benefit. This proposal addresses the last link without presuming that it succeeds.

## Question and exactly two proposed contrasts

Does a frozen rule using only the permitted public checkpoint improve final private-suite success over a competent fixed continuation, and over a declared independent-sampling controller? Before evaluation, freeze the population and family sampling, initial-prompt/checkpoint law, public eligibility, supported continuation recipes, receiver/service/decoding law, scoring contract, failure rules and remaining horizon. An evaluator or eligible independent-family frame is **not yet selected or validated by this proposal**.

Let `X` be the permitted public history, including one common public diagnostic record. Let `Q_a(X)` be mean final score under continuation recipe `a` and the fixed receiver/horizon. Train and freeze a deterministic selector `d(X)`. Comparator `b1` is one competent recipe **index** selected on development data and then fixed; it is not the unknown population-optimal fixed arm. A fixed whole recipe may render task- or public-status-dependent bytes while retaining one declared identity across histories. The [action-unit correction](policy_action_unit_correction_20260924.md) makes explicit that E12's N1 and S1 are both same-prefix whole-recipe indices, although S1's three literal strings lack separate cross-status assignment; R1 drops the prior answer. If S1 is chosen as `b1`, do not add it again as an independent third benchmark. Comparator `b2` is a separately frozen independent-sampling/public-check controller under the declared deployment budget, with its information and stopping rule explicit. Its outcome mean is `Q_b2(X)`, whether or not it is represented as a single recipe. Identities, tuning rules and tie breaking remain to be frozen.

**Prospective update, 25 September (LEAD-POLICY-12 contract repair):** [LEAD-POLICY-11](policy_b2_one_redraw_decision_20260925.md) chooses B2-1R at the algorithm level: STOP when every case in the nonempty frozen public-case list passes; otherwise redraw once from the bare public task and return that result. E12/E14 currently have one public case per root; private assertions are excluded. This uses the initial diagnostic, so its end-to-end ledger includes it. The complete trial identity remains unfrozen because the task/source, receiver/evaluator, seed, endpoint, family and resource contracts are still open. B2-1R is the separate practical controller, not another same-prefix wording arm.

For the proposed primary prompt-choice comparison, `d` and `b1` each use one continuation from the same checkpoint and the same supported recipe set. STOP is an additional descriptive baseline. If a future design lets `d` stop selectively, it must explicitly change the claim to a prompting-and-stopping policy comparison. Restart likewise compares a context/instruction package, not wording alone.

For family `g`, use a prespecified within-family root average, with nonnegative weights `v_gi` summing to one. The two targets, conditional on frozen training/development, are

`theta_j = E_family[sum_i v_gi {Q_d(X_gi) - Q_bj(X_gi)}], j=1,2`.

Thus the proposed population weights families equally. A task-weighted population, unequal/random family weights or a convenience roster requires a different declared target and sampling argument. The initial-history distribution is shared across policies: these are landmark values, not automatically the value of repeatedly deploying `d` through a longer conversation. See [landmark theory, sections 2, 6 and 7](landmark_prompt_theory.md).

## Isolation, direct evaluation and costs

Keep every related task family, all its roots, prefixes, branches and labels in one of **training, tuning or untouched evaluation**. Freeze `d`, both comparators, feature access and every analysis choice before evaluation labels. Existing E11/E12 families and inspected source variants cannot become untouched evaluation families. Do not choose a learner, interval method, endpoint, subgroup or split because E12 outcomes look favorable. Any later outcome-informed amendment is labeled development and precedes a new evaluation freeze.

At each evaluation root, commit `d(X)` and the fixed recipe before viewing continuation scores, restore the identical checkpoint and directly execute the frozen policies with prespecified replicate counts and seed laws. The adaptive comparator `b2` may use its own subsequent deployment-visible outputs and public checks only under its already-frozen rule. Shared action branches can supply both policies' scores when their complete intervention laws coincide. Otherwise execute each strategy. Randomized scheduling alone is not an action propensity. Average paired policy differences within roots and then families; seeds are not independent families. Frozen replicate counts, correct marginal branch laws, receiver stability and branch isolation are required. Private grades and other experimental/counterfactual branch results may not inform any deployed decision; the comparator may use its own authorized public within-policy feedback. Neither that feedback nor any evaluation outcome may retune `d`, the comparators or their hyperparameters. An observed maximum over branch outcomes is a clairvoyant finite-bank summary, not evidence for the value of a deployable public-history rule, and a nonzero marginal arm effect is **not** an admission gate: useful opposite conditional effects can cancel.

Give every policy the same available public diagnostic bytes for the landmark quality comparison and prohibit private-grade access; a comparator may ignore information. Keep separate ledgers for actual research expenditure, incremental deployment cost **after** the shared checkpoint, and end-to-end deployment cost **from the task**. Charge an operation once to a hypothetical policy only when that frozen policy or its eligibility rule would require it: the same-prefix selector and fixed comparator need the initial diagnostic, while a bare-task redraw controller on an unconditional task population could ignore and skip it. If eligibility requires the diagnostic, charge it to every policy that must establish eligibility. [LEAD-POLICY-03](policy_cost_counterfactual_contract_20260924.md) records this correction. One common continuation-call ceiling does **not** make tokens, latency or total cost identical. Freeze a common total-resource allowance from a stated starting line and the controller's admissible use of it before a practical comparison; do not force a cheap comparator to waste resources. Without that contract, `theta_1` remains a quality mechanism contrast and no cost-efficiency claim follows. Formal cost hypotheses would require their own declared inference/multiplicity plan.

## Decisions and a conservative inference fallback

Retain the lead's [working usefulness threshold](worker_issues_resolution_20260921.md): `g=.05` absolute private-suite success for `theta_1`, meaning a .05 gain under the declared equal-family target, not necessarily five additional successes per 100 unweighted tasks. This is a research target, not measured user utility. At one fixed final analysis, use prespecified valid intervals `[L_j,U_j]`. For `theta_1`, **useful benefit** requires `L_1>.05`; **useful-gain futility** requires `U_1<.05`; otherwise the result is inconclusive about usefulness. Equality passes neither strict rule. `L_1>0` is a separate superiority label, which can coexist with useful-gain futility. For the second contrast, `L_2>0` supports a quality advantage over `b2`; `U_2<0` supports disadvantage. An unresolved second contrast or cost contract prevents a broader practical-superiority claim. No interim outcome-driven stopping or evaluation reuse is allowed.

A valid conservative fallback is available if the `n` complete-data family contrasts `D_gj` are independent across families, equally weighted and bounded in `[-1,1]`, conditional on the frozen policies, and their mean has expectation `theta_j` under the declared sampling design. For each contrast, Hoeffding gives

`P(|mean_g D_gj - theta_j| > r) <= 2 exp(-n r^2/2)`.

A union bound across **exactly these two contrasts** gives simultaneous coverage at least `.95` for `[mean D_j-r, mean D_j+r]`, intersected with `[-1,1]`, using

`r = sqrt(2 log(4/.05)/n) = sqrt(2 log(80)/n)`.

Independence between the two contrasts is unnecessary. This is the established bounded-family argument in the [inference review](landmark_inference_review_20260920.md), not a new theorem. It spends `.025` error on each two-sided interval and is more conservative than the earlier single-contrast 95% planning convention; power must be recalculated for the selected procedure. Unknown grades are not silently zero or deleted: use the prespecified failure definition or pathwise missing-outcome bounds with the reviewed endpoint construction.

Independent scalar arithmetic gives `2 log(80)/.05^2 = 3505.6213077391044`, hence **3,506 independent families** for `r<=.05`; radii at 3,506 and 3,505 are `.04999730` and `.05000443`. This guarantees a radius, not 80% power, attainable benefit or feasibility. This radius calculation does not establish 80% power under a true gain of `.10`. A hypothetical 14-independent-family radius is `.791204`; E12 does not supply that sampling contract and receives no such interval. More repetitions per existing family do not increase `n`.

## What must be settled next

Before a trial freeze, resolve the sampling frame/family dependence, endpoint validity, public information, policy and comparator identities, family/root weights, missingness, total cost, repetitions, inference procedure and resource ceiling together. Assess precision and power for the declared useful-benefit test using explicit paired-family variance scenarios; do not infer them from the outcome-selected opportunities or the seven-root pilot. A more efficient proposed interval needs justified assumptions and prospective validation. The [known-assignment inference note](known_assignment_inference_20260921.md) supplies conditional asymptotic results for off-policy scores, not finite-sample calibration here; direct paired evaluation does not require DR.

**Separate proposed implementation cap, not execution authority:** after lead review and a committed numerical/source freeze, at most one CPU worker and ten wall-clock minutes for source/mock checks and a bounded synthetic comparison of declared inference procedures; zero receiver/model calls, benchmark/reference/candidate execution, installs or paid spend. Any such simulation must label its laws, seeds, Monte Carlo precision and limitations. No simulation, policy training or real collection was run for this memo; only the displayed scalar arithmetic was evaluated. Current E12 authority is unchanged.

Internal review: an independent design author, coordinating lead and a separate evidence reviewer checked the target, comparator information, family-mean alignment and arithmetic. Corrections distinguish equal-family from unweighted-task gains, require iid target-aligned family contrasts, and allow the adaptive comparator its own permitted public feedback. No empirical effect or new foundational theorem is asserted.
