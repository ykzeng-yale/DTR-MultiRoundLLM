## What this shows (descriptive, finite roster)

1. **Unlike E11, repair was possible here:** 4 of 14 roots failed at STOP. Neutral continuation, with or
   without the executed public diagnostic (N0, N1), **reproduced STOP exactly on every root**: no repair and
   no damage.
2. **The primary contrast is essentially null (−0.036),** made of one gain (842) and one loss (863).
3. **Arms that change behaviour trade repair against damage.** The directive S1 and context-removal R1 were
   the only arms that repaired anything (1 and 2 of 8), and they also caused the most damage (2 and 3 of 20).
   None was net positive against STOP.
4. **Informative observation, not a claim:**
   - Root 863's initial answer **failed its public example but passed both private assertions**.
   - S1, instructed to fix that public failure, broke the private verdict in both replicates.
   - So a diagnostic can direct change toward a public case in a way the thin private suite scores as
     damage. That is a measurement-and-contract interaction to examine, not evidence against diagnostics as
     such.
5. **The public diagnostic tracks private status closely here** (13 of 14 roots agree). That makes it a
   plausible decision-time signal for *when* to intervene. Whether any policy using it beats STOP is not
   tested here and would need the separate validation design.

**Scope:** 14 roots from a provisional duplicate screen, not independent families; two private assertions per
root; one run. No efficacy or futility claim. The optional MRL-17 exploratory static descriptions can follow
separately and do not alter this report.
