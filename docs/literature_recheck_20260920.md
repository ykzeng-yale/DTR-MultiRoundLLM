# Independent recheck of load-bearing literature claims

Audit date: 2026-09-20. Scope: the ten newly emphasized arXiv identifiers, the resulting novelty claims in `docs/literature_audit.md` and `COORDINATION.md`, and one directly relevant omitted randomized study. This is a source and reasoning audit, not a replication of the cited experiments or a new exhaustive literature review. The earlier audit remains historical; the corrections below should control interpretation where it conflicts with them. Line references describe the repository at inspection and may move.

**Finding:** all ten identifiers resolve to real papers with the relevant titles. The warning that longitudinal causal prompting has substantial precedent is correct. Several stronger claims in the newer audit are unsupported or mathematically incorrect. Neither an absence search nor an empirical failure of one estimator implementation establishes the claimed impossibility or priority. A scoped empirical contribution remains possible; its success requires completed validation.

## 2. Corrections to load-bearing claims

**A. “No such paper exists” is not an evidence status.** Audit line 42 labels stochastic interventions involving text `NOT_FOUND`, then concludes absence. Nakamura–Imai's dynamic paper explicitly contains that topic. Replace with: “Feature-level dynamic stochastic interventions for generated unstructured data exist. We have not established whether an earlier paper treats the precise interactive generated-slate estimand and validation design proposed here.” A narrower negative search remains unresolved, not a priority result.

**B. Strings have well-defined probabilities and support.** Audit line 113 says support has no definition when an action is a string. For finite token strings, the action space is countable; a conditional mass function `b(a|h)` and absolute-continuity requirement `pi(a|h)>0 => b(a|h)>0` are ordinary probability objects. With appropriate termination, token probabilities induce a string distribution. Practical support, inaccessible probabilities, unstable weights, and unidentified responses are serious problems; they do not make these objects undefined. Continuous-action differentiability or invertibility theorems cannot simply be imported for strings, but that is a different claim. This correction is mathematical reasoning, not a novelty assertion.

**C. Coarsening does not automatically identify an effect.** Audit lines 193–214 present coarsening as creating the propensity and solving support. Both are overstated. If `Z=f(A)` is a semantic category, a causal intervention on `Z` must specify how exact text versions are generated, or assume a justified version-irrelevance condition. Positive category counts do not guarantee conditional positivity, no hidden confounding, or transport to new text realizations. The current theory's exact candidate slate and randomized index avoid assuming all category members have equal effects. This is a design choice with explicit assumptions, not proof of a new general coarsening theorem.

**D. Past history is not automatically current-treatment contamination.** Audit lines 97, 113, and 174 conflate embedding an entire treated text with embedding a strictly pre-action history. Define `H_t` before `A_t`; then `f(H_t)` cannot contain the future realized `A_t` by construction. It can contain prior treatments and their consequences, as a sequential analysis generally requires. Problems arise when an embedding leaks the current action/outcome, loses confounder information, or when a static regression controls for mediators while targeting a total earlier-action effect. Unconditionally residualizing history against treatment is not a general remedy and can discard legitimate confounders. The inspected paper about point-treatment representations does not establish that remedy for this design.

**E. A geometric-mean token ratio is not an exact policy likelihood ratio.** For a string with token ratios `r_1,...,r_m`, its full likelihood ratio is their product, including the actual decoding/termination law. Replacing that product by its `m`th root generally changes the weighting functional; it is not interchangeable with exact IPW/DR for the same target. A training stabilization heuristic may be a useful baseline, but should not silently replace a causal estimator. Exact ratios for a prospectively randomized selector over complete strings are legitimate without token ratios when generator cancellation conditions hold.

**F. Do not erase estimands in predecessors.** Audit line 86 should say “no separate explicit identification theorem located in the inspected formulation,” not “no estimand.” Zhang–Wang–Dhillon 2025 state potential outcomes and a DTR objective. CausalCollab goes further and supplies identification assumptions and results. Accordingly, neither “first multi-round causal prompting formulation” nor “only the identification layer is ours” survives these sources.

## 3. Additional check on the ADWM argument

The repository treats this source as decisive against token OPE (audit lines 113, 138, 157). The [v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05558v2), Appendix F.3, describes greedy temperature-zero data collection; Appendix G.1 obtains token log-probabilities by rerunning the models. A teacher-forced softmax probability is not necessarily the assignment probability of a greedy decoder or of the full prompt/model mixture. Therefore, labeling these “exact behavior propensities” requires a decoding-law audit that this repository has not performed. This concern does not reproduce or invalidate all reported comparisons.

There is also an independently checkable issue in §4.1, equation (2), PDF p.5. It defines `q_alpha(tau) ∝ p_theta(tau) product_t pi_e(a_t|h_t)^alpha`, after dropping the behavior denominator, then asserts recovery of the target law when `p_theta=p_b` and `alpha=1`. **Our counterexample:** one step, actions `{0,1}`, `b=(0.9,0.1)`, `pi=(0.5,0.5)`. The displayed tilt normalizes to `(0.9,0.1)`, not `(0.5,0.5)`. For outcome `1[A=1]`, values are 0.1 versus 0.5. The exact change of measure uses `pi/b`. This refutes that recovery claim as printed, not every possible world-model estimator. The formula and assertion were checked in the PDF, rather than inferred from HTML conversion.

## 4. “Nobody randomized multi-turn” needs a much narrower statement

A primary omitted example is [Louie, Converse, Yang, and Brunskill, *Micro-Randomized Trial of an AI-Simulated Practice Tool for Therapeutic Skills*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v339/louie26a.html), PMLR 339:43–69, 28 June 2026. The study repeatedly randomizes post-practice AI feedback and reflection conditions in an LLM-patient practice system, with 25 students over five weeks. This is a deployed longitudinal MRT involving an LLM system. It is not a within-conversation randomized next-prompt selector, and this check did not verify export of per-decision propensity logs.

Consequently:

- A blanket claim that no repeated randomized LLM deployment/MRT exists is false.
- C3 also samples alternative actions at restored histories, while Fresh–Shin explicitly discusses message-level randomization; neither should be described as a completed instance of our exact deployment without checking its design.
- “No earlier study has the exact combination of randomized within-conversation selector, retained propensities, targeted history-conditional effects, and prospective OPE validation” remains **unestablished**, not verified true.
- Logging probabilities is an essential reproducibility property. It is not, by itself, a new statistical contribution or a justified “first” claim. Randomized bandit experiments can support causal claims when the design and estimand warrant them; “bandit” and “causal” are not mutually exclusive categories.

Remove the “first prospectively randomized ...” sentence at audit lines 230–233 and the unqualified absence statements near audit lines 176, 207, 242 and COORDINATION lines 132–134. Their replacement should describe the actual experiment and contribution without priority language.

## 5. Defensible positioning for the current project

Suggested language:

> Building on longitudinal human–LM causal analysis, language-valued DTR learning, contextual off-policy prompt optimization, and conversational estimand work, we study personalized prompt selection over multiple rounds with a frozen receiver. A frozen generator produces exact candidate strings before a randomized selector acts. The design records the complete candidate slate, selection probabilities, histories, costs, and outcomes. We evaluate conditional continuation effects and policy value using established longitudinal causal estimators, and test their calibration and policy improvements against independently executed randomized continuations and fresh trajectories. The scientific contribution sought is a validated measurement and evaluation framework for this specific intervention design; classical g-formula, IPW, and doubly robust identities are not presented as new theory.

“Conditional” here means an expectation among histories or represented histories under specified assumptions, not the unobservable individual effect of one realized conversation. Local branching validates local continuations, while whole-policy evaluation requires the target history distribution. Generated-slate selector ratios apply only under the stipulated shared generator; changing the generator changes the intervention law and can require new data or additional density ratios. These claims are compatible with the current theory and give falsifiable experimental objectives. The primary-source review alone does not establish publishable novelty, empirical improvement, or submission readiness.
